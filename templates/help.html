What is a Chanda (छन्दस्)?

Sanskrit prosody or chandaśāstra (छन्दशास्त्र) is the systematic study of poetic meters and verse structures in Sanskrit. Meters are broadly classified into Akṣara-vṛtta (fixed syllabic length and gaṇa structure) and Mātrā-vṛtta (fixed moraic count).

Rules for Laghu and Guru Marking

Every Sanskrit syllable (akṣara) is scanned as either Laghu (ल / Light / 1 mātrā) or Guru (ग / Heavy / 2 mātrās):

Category Rule / Vowels Weight & Mora
Laghu (ल) Short vowels: अ, इ, उ, ऋ, ऌ (not followed by conjunct/anusvara/visarga) 1 Mātrā (Light)
Guru (ग) Long vowels: आ, ई, ऊ, ॠ, ॡ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Short vowel followed by Anusvāra (ं) or Visarga (ः) 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Short vowel followed by a Saṃyuktākṣara (consonant conjunct / halanta cluster) 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Last syllable of a poetic quarter (pāda-anta) optionally scans as Guru 2 Mātrās (Heavy)

The 8 Traditional Gaṇas (अष्टगणाः)

In classical Sanskrit metrics, syllables are grouped into triads (groups of 3) yielding 23 = 8 gaṇas:

म-या-रा-स्त-रा-ज-भा-न-स-ल-गं • "यमाताराजभानसलगम्"
Gaṇa (गण) Pattern (ल-ग) Mnemonic Word Gaṇa (गण) Pattern (ल-ग) Mnemonic Word
य (Y) लगग (LGG) यमाता भ (B) गलल (GLL) भानस
र (R) गलग (GLG) राजभा ज (J) लगल (LGL) जभान
त (T) गगल (GGL) ताराज स (S) ललग (LLG) सलगा
न (N) ललल (LLL) नसल म (M) गगग (GGG) मातारा

Understanding Fuzzy Matches & Annotations

When a line contains a typo, irregular sandhi, or slight variant, Chandojñānam uses sequence alignment to find the closest candidate meters with concrete transformation markers:

  • i(ल) or i(ग) : Insertion of a missing Laghu or Guru syllable.
  • r(syllable)[ल/ग]{suggestion} : Replacement / vowel alteration needed on that syllable.
  • d(syllable) : Deletion of an extra syllable.
