What is a Chanda (छन्दस्)?

Sanskrit prosody or Chandaśāstra (छन्दशास्त्र) is the systematic science of poetic meters and verse structures in Sanskrit. Meters are broadly classified into Akṣara-vṛtta (fixed syllabic length and gaṇa structure) and Mātrā-vṛtta (fixed moraic count).

The Pādānta-Guru Rule (पादस्यान्ते द्विरूपत्वम्)

सानुस्वारश्च दीर्घश्च विसर्गी च गुरुर्भवेत् । वर्णः संयोगपूर्वश्च तथा पादान्तगोऽपि वा ॥

In classical Sanskrit prosody, the last syllable (akṣara) of a poetic quarter (pāda) or verse has the special privilege of dvirūpatva (dual status):

  • If the last syllable ends in a natural short vowel (Laghu), it can optionally or by rule function as a Guru (ग) to satisfy the meter's requirement.
  • Visual Indication in विशालवृत्तावलिः: To maintain phonetic honesty and prosodic clarity, such syllables are highlighted with a distinct purple badge and marked as ल* (गुरु).

Metrical Rhythm Synthesizer (ताल-ध्वनिः)

Clicking the Play Rhythm button synthesizes real-time percussive tones using the Web Audio API:

  • Laghu (ल): 1 Mātrā (short, crisp high-tone stroke).
  • Guru (ग): 2 Mātrās (resonant, deep bass stroke).
  • The scansion table dynamically highlights each syllable in sync with the beat.

What is an Upajāti (उपजाति)?

An Upajāti is a versatile hybrid meter formed when quarters (pādas) of closely related meters are intermixed within a single 4-line verse.

The most celebrated Upajāti is the 11-syllable Triṣṭubh mixture of Indravajrā (starts with Guru: गगल गगल लगल गग) and Upendravajrā (starts with Laghu: लगल गगल लगल गग). Since each of the 4 padas can independently start with either Laghu or Guru, there are $2^4 = 16$ traditional named varieties of Upajāti (*Kīrti*, *Vāṇī*, *Mālā*, *Śālā*, *Haṃsī*, *Māyā*, *Kamalā*, etc.).

विशालवृत्तावलिः automatically detects Upajāti verses and displays the exact subtype and pāda-by-pāda breakdown.

How to use the Poetic Composition Assistant (काव्यसहायकः)?

The काव्यसहायकः (Composition Studio) tab is designed for poets, scholars, and students writing original Sanskrit verses:

  1. Choose a target meter (e.g. Mandākrāntā, Vasantatilakā, Indravajrā).
  2. Type each pada into the 4-pāda workspace.
  3. The system provides instant visual feedback:
    • Green slot: Syllable weight matches the meter blueprint.
    • Red slot: Syllable weight mismatch (e.g. typed Laghu where Guru is required).
    • Pulsing slot: Displays the prompt for the Next required syllable weight (ल or ग).
  4. When all 4 padas are validated, copy or export your completed verse.

Rules for Laghu and Guru Marking

Category Rule / Vowels Weight & Mora
Laghu (ल) Short vowels: अ, इ, उ, ऋ, ऌ (not followed by conjunct/anusvara/visarga) 1 Mātrā (Light)
Guru (ग) Long vowels: आ, ई, ऊ, ॠ, ॡ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Short vowel followed by Anusvāra (ं) or Visarga (ः) 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Short vowel followed by a Saṃyuktākṣara (consonant cluster / halanta) 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
Guru (ग) Last syllable of a poetic quarter (pāda-anta) optionally scans as Guru 2 Mātrās (Heavy)
