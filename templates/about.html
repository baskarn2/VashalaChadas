About Sanskrit Prosody (छन्दशास्त्रम्) & विशालवृत्तावलिः

Sanskrit prosody or chandaśāstra (छन्दशास्त्र) is the traditional study and science of poetic meters and verse structures in Sanskrit. The chandas have their own distinct rhythm, movements, and aesthetics. Sanskrit meters include those based on a fixed number of syllables per verse (akṣara-vṛtta), and those based on a fixed number of morae per verse (mātrā-vṛtta).

Every akṣara (syllable) is identified to be either laghu (short / 1 mora) or guru (long / 2 morae) as per the rules of chandaśāstra. Thus, every line of Sanskrit text can be transformed into a binary sequence of laghu and guru markers. Specific patterns of such sequences are identified as various chandas.

विशालवृत्तावलिः (Viśālavṛttāvaliḥ) has a comprehensive database of over 200+ chandas collated from classic authoritative sources, most notable among which are Vṛttaratnākara (वृत्तरत्नाकर) and Śabdakalpadruma (शब्दकल्पद्रुम).

Comprehensive Meter Classifications (छन्दो-वर्गीकरणम्)

Sanskrit meters are broadly classified into two principal categories, with further traditional subdivisions:

1. Akṣaragaṇa-vṛtta (अक्षरगणवृत्तम् / वर्णवृत्तम्) — Syllabic Meters

Meters governed by a fixed number of syllables per quarter (pāda) and a strict sequence of laghu and guru syllables grouped into standard triads (gaṇas):

  • Sama-vṛtta (समवृत्तम्): All four quarters (pādas 1, 2, 3, and 4) possess identical syllabic length and identical gaṇa sequences.
    Examples: Anuṣṭubh (8), Indravajrā (11), Upendravajrā (11), Śālinī (11), Rathoddhatā (11), Vaṃśastha (12), Drutavilambita (12), Bhujaṅgaprayāta (12), Toṭaka (12), Vasantatilakā (14), Mālinī (15), Śikhariṇī (17), Mandākrāntā (17), Hariṇī (17), Pṛthvī (17), Śārdūlavikrīḍita (19), Sragdharā (21).
  • Ardhasama-vṛtta (अर्धसमवृत्तम्): The odd quarters (pādas 1 and 3) share one metric structure, while the even quarters (pādas 2 and 4) follow a differing structure.
    Examples: Viyoginī (10 + 11 syllables), Puṣpitāgrā (12 + 13 syllables), Vegavatī, Aparavaktra.
  • Viṣama-vṛtta (विषमवृत्तम्): Meters where all four quarters have differing syllabic lengths or gaṇa arrangements.
    Examples: Udgata, Saurabhaka, Lalitā, Amṛtadhārā.
  • Upajāti (उपजाति): Versatile hybrid verses formed by intermixing related meters across the four quarters.
    Most prominently, the 16 classical varieties of 11-syllable Triṣṭubh Upajāti mixing Indravajrā and Upendravajrā (such as Kīrti, Vāṇī, Mālā, Śālā, Haṃsī, Māyā, Kamalā), and 12-syllable Jagatī Upajāti mixing Vaṃśastha and Indravaṃśā.
2. Mātrā-vṛtta (मात्रिकच्छन्दः / जातिः) — Moraic Meters

Meters regulated by total moraic counts (mātrās) per quarter rather than fixed individual syllable sequences. In these meters, short syllables count as 1 mātrā and long syllables count as 2 mātrās:

  • Āryā (आर्या): The preeminent moraic meter of Sanskrit lyrical and philosophical literature.
    Structure: 1st quarter has 12 mātrās, 2nd has 18 mātrās, 3rd has 12 mātrās, and 4th has 15 mātrās (total 57 mātrās). Subvarieties include Gīti, Upagīti, Udgīti, and Āryāgīti.
  • Vaitālīya (वैतालीय) & Aupacchandasika (औपच्छन्दसिक): Fixed moraic counts per pada with defined concluding cadence.
